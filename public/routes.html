Route lab: draw a play, see how open it really gets.

Put five receivers on the board and give each a route. Pick the coverage, or a real 2022 defense. The simulator runs the play a thousand times using how open those routes actually got against that coverage in NFL player-tracking data, and an AI coordinator will argue with you about it.

Routes are drawn as the average path of every such route in the data. Each X is where the matched defender actually stood at the snap in that coverage. Drag a circle to move a receiver.

How it works

For every receiver on your board, the simulator looks up how much separation that route produced against that coverage at the moment the ball was thrown, across every time it was run in the 2022 data, and samples from that distribution. Where you line up matters: outside receivers and slot receivers have different tables. Picking a real team shifts every receiver by how much tighter or looser that defense played than the league. Picking a real receiver shifts his row by how much more open than expected he got that season.

Then a quarterback has to throw it. A target-choice model fitted on the same plays picks a receiver given each one's openness and depth. A completion model and an interception model, fitted on the throws, resolve the pass. Yards after the catch are sampled by route. Sacks and scrambles happen at the rates that coverage actually produced. Every outcome is converted to expected points for the down, distance and field position you set, so a play that gains four yards on third and three scores better than one that gains eight on third and twelve.

The coordinator is Claude. It can run the simulator itself, and it is told not to quote any number it did not get from a run. Everything on the results panel comes from arithmetic, not from the model.

The data, and what the simulator does not know

NFL Next Gen Stats player tracking released for the Big Data Bowl 2025, weeks 1 to 9 of the 2022 season, with routes and coverages charted by Pro Football Focus. Licensed CC BY-NC 4.0; this is a non-commercial project. The tables cover receiver snaps on dropbacks.

The simulator does not model pass protection, play action, motion, specific defenders, route combinations that stress one defender, or the quarterback's eyes. Target choice in real data is only weakly explained by openness (the fitted model gets the target right about a third of the time against a one-in-five baseline), so target shares here are the noisiest number on the page. Treat the separation and completion figures as the solid part and the target shares as a rough guide.
